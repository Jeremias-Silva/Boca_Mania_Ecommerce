// XENEIZE TEMPLE OF GLORY

1977 Copa Libertadores Final

Boca Juniors faced Cruzeiro in a historic continental battle at the Estadio Centenario. After two closely contested matches, the tie was decided on penalties. The aggregate score ended 1–1. In the shootout, Hugo Gatti became the hero by saving Vanderlei’s decisive penalty. With this victory, Boca Juniors won its first Copa Libertadores and secured its place in South American football history.

Boca Juniors 1 x 0 Cruzeiro

(won on penalties 5–4)
Team Lineup: Gatti; Pernía, Tesare, Mouzo, Tarantini; J. J. Benítez (Ribolzi), Suñé, Zanabria; Mastrángelo, Veglio (Pavón), Felman.
Coach: Juan Carlos Lorenzo.
1977 Intercontinental Cup

Boca Juniors stepped onto European soil with the identity of a champion and never looked back. From the opening minutes, the team imposed total dominance, controlling the rhythm, the space, and the game itself against Borussia Mönchengladbach. With relentless pressure and clinical execution, Boca built a commanding lead that defined the night. The 3–0 scoreline reflected not just victory, but authority — a statement of power on the world stage. Every moment carried the weight of La Bombonera, even thousands of kilometers away. It was more than a match. It was the confirmation of global supremacy.

Boca Juniors 3 x 0 Borussia Mönchengladbach
Team Lineup: Gatti; Pernía, Tesare, Bordón, Suárez; Salinas, Suñé, Zanabria; Mastrángelo, Saldaño (Veglio), Felman.
Coach: Juan Carlos Lorenzo.
1978 Copa Libertadores Final

Boca Juniors confirmed its continental dominance with a historic back-to-back triumph. Inside an electrified La Bombonera, the team delivered a devastating 4–0 victory over Deportivo Cali. From the first whistle, Boca imposed total control, intensity, and precision. The night became a showcase of pure Xeneize superiority in South America. With this win, Boca secured its second consecutive Copa Libertadores title and reinforced its legacy as a continental giant.

Boca Juniors 4 x 0 Deportivo Cali
Team Lineup: Gatti; Pernía, Sá, Mouzo, Bordón; Benítez, Suñé, Zanabria; Mastrángelo, Salinas, Perotti.
Coach: Juan Carlos Lorenzo.
2000 Copa Libertadores Final

Boca Juniors completed a historic resurrection under Carlos Bianchi, facing Palmeiras in one of the most intense finals in South American football. The first leg at La Bombonera ended 2–2, followed by a tense 0–0 draw at the Morumbi Stadium in São Paulo. With the aggregate locked at 2–2, the title was decided on penalties. Óscar Córdoba became the decisive figure, delivering crucial saves in the shootout, while Juan Román Riquelme controlled the rhythm and identity of the team throughout the series. Boca triumphed 4–2 on penalties, reclaiming continental glory and igniting a new golden era.

Boca Juniors 0 x 0 Palmeiras

(won on penalties 4–2)
Córdoba; Ibarra, Bermúdez, Samuel, Arruabarrena; Battaglia, Traverso, Basualdo; Riquelme; Guillermo Barros Schelotto, Palermo.
Coach: Carlos Bianchi
2000 Intercontinental Cup

Boca Juniors arrived in Tokyo and delivered one of the most iconic performances in club history against Real Madrid. Martín Palermo struck twice in the opening six minutes, shocking the “Galácticos” and giving Boca immediate control of the final. From that moment on, the team managed the match with intensity, discipline, and total confidence on the world stage. Juan Román Riquelme controlled the rhythm of the game, shielding possession under constant pressure and dictating the tempo. Final score: Boca Juniors 2–1 Real Madrid. A historic victory that confirmed Boca as world champions once again.

Boca Juniors 2 x 1 Real Madrid
Córdoba; Ibarra, Bermúdez, Traverso, Matellán; Battaglia, Serna, Basualdo; Riquelme; Delgado, Palermo.
Coach: Carlos Bianchi
2001 Copa Libertadores Final

Boca Juniors entered the final against Cruz Azul with the weight of defending their continental crown. After two tightly contested legs, the tie remained level and the decision once again went to penalties at a roaring La Bombonera. In a night defined by pressure and mentality, Óscar Córdoba stood tall, delivering crucial saves in the shootout. Boca remained composed under the highest tension, executing with precision when it mattered most. Final score (aggregate): 1–1 Penalty shootout: Boca Juniors 3–1 Cruz Azul With this victory, Boca secured back-to-back Copa Libertadores titles, reinforcing its dominance in South American football.

Boca Juniors 0 x 1 Cruz Azul

(won on penalties 3–1)
Córdoba; Ibarra, Bermúdez, Burdisso, Rodríguez; Villarreal, Traverso, Gaitán; Riquelme; Delgado, Palermo.
Coach: Carlos Bianchi
2003 Copa Libertadores Final

Boca Juniors delivered a complete tactical and emotional masterclass against Santos in the final. The first leg at La Bombonera ended 2–0, setting the tone with intensity, control, and precision. In the return leg at Vila Belmiro, Boca sealed the title with a 3–1 victory, showing full dominance across both matches. Over the two legs, Santos’ attacking structure was completely dismantled by Boca’s discipline and execution. Led by a brilliant young Carlos Tevez and the decisive presence of Marcelo Delgado, Boca controlled every phase of the series. Final aggregate score: Boca Juniors 5–1 Santos With this triumph, Boca lifted its fifth Copa Libertadores title and reaffirmed its place at the summit of South American football.

Boca Juniors 3 x 1 Santos
Abbondanzieri; Ibarra, Schiavi, Burdisso, C. Rodríguez; Cascini, Battaglia, Cagna; Tevez, Delgado, G. Barros Schelotto.
Coach: Carlos Bianchi
2003 Intercontinental Cup

Boca Juniors faced AC Milan in a tactical and physically demanding final, against a world-class squad featuring players like Kaká, Pirlo, and Shevchenko. It was a tight, high-intensity match defined by structure, discipline, and defensive resilience. Goalkeeper Roberto “Pato” Abbondanzieri delivered a decisive performance, making crucial saves that kept Boca in control under constant pressure. Boca held firm through the full 90 minutes and extra tension, securing the victory on penalties. Final score: Boca Juniors 1–1 AC Milan (Boca won 4–2 on penalties) With this triumph, Boca claimed its third Intercontinental Cup and reaffirmed its place at the summit of world football.

Boca Juniors 1 x 1 Milan

(won on penalties 4–2)
Abbondanzieri; Perea, Schiavi, Burdisso, Rodríguez; Cagna, Cascini, Battaglia, Donnet; Guillermo Barros Schelotto, Iarley.
Coach: Carlos Bianchi
2007 Copa Libertadores Final

Boca Juniors faced Grêmio in a decisive continental final defined by control, discipline, and elite execution across both legs. At La Bombonera, Boca delivered a commanding 3–0 performance, setting the tone with intensity and authority. In Porto Alegre, the team managed the game with tactical maturity, closing the series with a 2–0 victory away from home. Final aggregate score: Boca Juniors 5–0 Grêmio With this triumph, Boca secured its sixth Copa Libertadores title, closing one of the most dominant continental campaigns in modern football history.

Boca Juniors 2 x 0 Grêmio
Caranta; Ibarra, Díaz, Morel Rodríguez, Clemente Rodríguez; Ledesma, Banega, Cardozo; Riquelme; Palacio, Palermo.
Coach: Miguel Ángel Russo
